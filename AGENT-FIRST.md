# Agent-first editing, rules and shared packs

Decided 2026-09-17. This is the direction; the editor stays for fine adjustment, but the
video is *built* by the agent from a brief, a set of rules and a plan the human can read,
correct and approve. See [SPEC.md](./SPEC.md) for the shared-editor requirement, which
still governs everything here: every rule action runs through the same operations the UI
uses, and everything the agent decides is inspectable and editable in the UI.

Three tables count as ADRs, and only these: the Decided table below (1–…), the founding
stack table in [SPEC.md](./SPEC.md#decided) (S1–S10, 2026-09-16) and the harness table in
[HARNESS.md](./HARNESS.md#decisions) (H1–H10, 2026-09-21). There is no `docs/adr/`. A row is
amended, dated, when the code moves past it; it is never silently rewritten. The audit that
produced rows 75 onward is [docs/ADR-AUDIT.md](./docs/ADR-AUDIT.md).

## Decided

| # | Decision | Why | Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The plan is an editable artifact, not a log.** The agent writes `plan.json` per video: which template, which rules fired and why, which assets, which beats. The UI renders it, the human can change a choice ("use the other template") and regenerate. *Amended 2026-09-24: the plan lives in the EDL (`edl.plan`, `sequence.plan`), not in a `plan.json` per video; the run directory gets `project-plan.json` as the agent's scratch.* | Agent-first means the human reviews *decisions*, not keyframes. | 2 | **Rules = semantic condition + structured action.** `when` is free text the agent judges ("this clip is gameplay"); `then` is structured: apply template (with overrides), add asset at start/end, forbid/force overlay sources, extra prompt text. Every edit a rule makes carries `by: "rule:<id>"`, so re-applying is idempotent like templates. *Amended 2026-09-24: the marker is `template:<id>/rule:<ids>` (a rule acts through a template), see RULES.md.* | A rule that is entirely a prompt cannot be shown, tested or edited in the UI, which breaks parity. A rule that is entirely structured cannot express "is about the stream". | Free-text-only rules; tag-matching-only rules. |
| 3 | **Rules execute at generation and on "re-apply".** On ordinary agent messages they are constraints in the prompt, not re-executed. | Re-running twenty rules on "move the title" is slow and overwrites work. | Executing on every message. |
| 4 | **Classification is two-layered.** Project/source tags set by the human are ground truth; the agent adds per-clip tags in `clips.json` from transcript + frames. | 63 | **Renderer transitions are an item property, not a template section (2026-09-21).** A shot carries how it arrives over the one before it on its track; the overlap comes out of the video's length, never out of either shot's footage. | 64 | **A newly imported source transcribes itself, and that run never holds the project lock (2026-09-21).** It is a `transcribe-media` job written with status `background`, which `q.activeJob` does not see and `q.unfinishedJobs` does; where each source stands is a record on the media in the EDL (`media.transcription`), written by the shared `media.transcription` operation. | 65 | **A template authors the framing (2026-09-22).** `layout` carries the screen and camera rectangles as shares of the source frame, the share of height the camera takes and which half it is in; it is written onto every shot with footage and only when it differs from what is there. `mode: "source"` — every template that existed before — leaves each shot alone. | Framing is the loudest thing a look does, and it was decided per clip by the selection agent, from frames, unrepeatably: the same channel's two streams came back framed differently, and nothing could be written down. | 66 | **The layers a template holds across a video hold across the body, not the bookends (2026-09-22).** The hook, the cards and the watermark start when an intro ends and stop where an end card begins; `atFraction: 1` is the last frame before the card. The first real render put a hook question and a call to action on top of a card that says when the stream is — work a person would immediately undo by hand. A card arrives with its own sound, so a bed under it is two pieces of music at once. | Spanning everything (what it did); a per-layer flag (five flags to express one idea); ending the bed at the card's start only when the card has audio (invisible rule, different result per asset). |
| 67 | **The clip selection's edits are marked `select`, and a template replaces them (2026-09-22).** A clip cut out of a recording arrives with a hook title, dead-air cuts and push-ins written by the selection pass; those carried an empty author, which is what a person's own hand edit carries. | Applying a template then laid a second set on top: two cards at the top of the frame for three seconds, and two overlapping push-ins whose zooms multiply. A hand edit still survives both. | Leaving them unmarked (the bug); having templates skip shots that already carry edits (a template that cannot restyle a generated clip is useless); deleting them at publish time (throws away the only edit a clip has if no template is ever applied). |
| 68 | **The pace of the cuts is measured against a finished video, not chosen (2026-09-22).** `scripts/pace.ts` measures the distribution of pauses a video you already like keeps, and searches for the `minGapSec` and `keepSec` that reproduce it on your own material. | `stream-short` shipped cutting every gap over a third of a second to a tenth, which left no pause over a third of a second anywhere in the output — while the channel it was modelled on keeps one every three seconds. It cut the breathing along with the dead air, and no frame shows that; only the distribution does. The fit matches pauses rather than running time, and says so, because material with more thinking in it than the reference had needs a shorter threshold than the fit suggests. | Tuning by watching (what produced the wrong numbers); matching mean level or total silence removed (says nothing about how it feels); fitting automatically at apply time (a template whose pace changes with its material is not a look). |
| 69 | **A template can say how loud the finished video is, and never clips to get there (2026-09-22).** `audio.targetLufs` measures the footage over the part being used, places it at the target, and levels the bookends against where it now plays. The gain is capped by the footage's own peaks (a decibel below full scale) and by the timeline's own bound on a shot's volume. *Amended 2026-09-24: the target never turns a video down, and the ceiling is the footage's peaks; a body-of-signal ceiling was tried and reverted the same day because it reached −16.7 LUFS with peaks at 0.0 dB.* | A short is published into a feed that normalises to about -14 LUFS. Two four-hour streams measured -22, the channel's own published shorts -18, and what this produced -21.8 — quieter than the video before it, which on a phone is the difference between being heard and being scrolled past. Both the reference and the output have a 21dB crest factor, so the remaining gap is a microphone with no compressor on it: a limiter would close it and change how the voice sounds, which is not a template's decision to make silently. | Loudness normalisation at render with a limiter (changes the voice, and no way to hear it coming); normalising to peak (says nothing about how loud it sounds); leaving it alone (what it did). |
| 70 | **The clip selection's `hook` is the line a card holds (2026-09-22).** The prompt asks for ten words, a question or a claim, in the language being spoken — not the first sentence transcribed, which is what it asked for before. | The field is written onto a white card and held on screen, often for the whole video. Two of eight hooks off a real stream arrived as sentences that had to be cut to fit, and a sentence cut to fit loses its ending, which is usually the point.
